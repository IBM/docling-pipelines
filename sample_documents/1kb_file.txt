The history of computing is a story of human ingenuity, beginning with simple tools like the abacus. For millennia, calculating devices were mechanical—from the Antikythera mechanism of ancient Greece to Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine in the 19th century. Lady Ada Lovelace envisioned that these machines could do more than just crunch numbers; they could process any form of information. The 20th century brought the digital revolution. Alan Turing formalized the concepts of computation and algorithms, laying the groundwork for theoretical computer science. During World War II, machines like Colossus and ENIAC were built, using vacuum tubes to perform calculations at unprecedented speeds. The invention of the transistor transformed everything, leading to smaller, more reliable, and much faster computers. This paved the way for microprocessors and the personal computing era, making computers accessible to everyone.
